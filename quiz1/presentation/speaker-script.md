## Slide 22 — What Did 2025 Teach Us?

Tiempo: **3:30–4:00 minutos**, incluyendo los cuatro paneles interactivos.

Objetivo: presentar rápidamente las cuatro ideas y mostrar por qué ARC-NCA merece atención especial.

### Introducción de la slide

> What kinds of systems have actually been successful? The 2025 award papers offer very different answers: recursive networks, program search, compression and cellular dynamics. I will briefly explain the algorithm behind each one.

### Botón 1 — TRM

*Haz clic en la tarjeta TRM. Señala el flowchart de izquierda a derecha.*

> TRM stands for Tiny Recursion Model. It uses a network of only about seven million parameters, but reuses that network many times.
>
> The input is x, the current answer is y, and z is an internal scratchpad. The network updates z while looking at the problem and its current answer, then uses z to revise y. Through deep supervision, it learns to improve an imperfect answer instead of solving everything in one attempt.
>
> The important lesson for us is that repeated correction may be more valuable than simply increasing the number of parameters.

*Cierra el panel.*

Apoyo en el paper: Figure 1 y Figure 3 de `1-Less is More- Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks.pdf`.

### Botón 2 — SOAR

*Haz clic en SOAR.*

> SOAR treats program synthesis as evolution. A language model generates candidate Python programs and executes them on the demonstrations. Failed programs are discarded, while promising ones are revised using error feedback.
>
> SOAR also saves useful search attempts as examples for fine-tuning the language model. Better search produces better training data, and better training data produces better search.
>
> This is different from asking an LLM for one answer. The system explores, tests and learns from its own attempts.

*Cierra el panel.*

Apoyo en el paper: Figure 1 de `2-Self_Improving_Language.pdf`.

### Botón 3 — CompressARC

*Haz clic en CompressARC.*

> CompressARC treats reasoning as compression. If one short explanation reconstructs every example, it probably captures the underlying rule.
>
> It has only seventy-six thousand parameters and no conventional pretraining. For each puzzle, a small network samples a latent code, reconstructs the known grids and balances reconstruction error against description length. The optimization happens only for the current puzzle.
>
> The missing test output is then generated by the compact representation learned from that single puzzle. The lesson is that a strong simplicity bias can produce generalization even without a large pretrained model.

*Cierra el panel.*

Apoyo en el paper: Figure 2, Algorithm 3 y Figure 3 de `3-ARC_AGI_Without_Pretraining.pdf`.

### Botón 4 — ARC-NCA

*Haz clic en ARC-NCA. Dedica más tiempo a este panel.*

> ARC-NCA interested us the most. It represents the grid as cells with a visible state, such as color, and hidden states that store local information.
>
> Every cell applies the same neural update rule. In EngramNCA, GeneCA develops low-level patterns, while GenePropCA regulates and propagates private memory. Repeating these stages lets a global pattern emerge from local interactions.
>
> The visible result is compared with the target using pixel-wise error. Backpropagation through time changes the local rule using the demonstrations, and the learned dynamics are then applied to the unseen test input.
>
> This is especially interesting from a physics perspective: the computation is not only a function evaluation. It is the trajectory of a high-dimensional dynamical system.

*Cierra el panel.*

Apoyo en el paper: Figures 2–4 y la sección “Training” de `7-ARC-NCA.pdf`.

### Cierre de la slide

> All four methods maintain a hypothesis, compare it with evidence and refine it. We chose NCA because it combines refinement with local dynamics and emergent computation.

## Slide 23 — How Does ARC-NCA Work?

Tiempo: **1:30 minutos**.

Objetivo: explicar NCA con lenguaje accesible antes de presentar la estrategia del equipo.

> A cellular automaton is a grid where each cell updates itself using nearby information. A Neural Cellular Automaton replaces the hand-written update rule with a small neural network.
>
> The equation says that the next state of cell i depends on its present state and its neighbors. The same function is used by every cell at every time step, which keeps the rule small and spatially consistent.
>
> Visible channels determine the ARC color; hidden channels act as local memory. In EngramNCA, this memory can encode useful primitives, while neighboring cells communicate when and where to activate them.

*Señala las tres matrices de izquierda a derecha.*

> This animation is conceptual: repeated local updates produce an organized global pattern. No central cell directs the process; the solution emerges from collective dynamics.

> The paper trains a new automaton for each problem using only its demonstrations. We can view this as task-time program synthesis: the learned dynamical rule is the program.

Apoyo en el paper: “NCA Models”, “From ARC to NCA” y Figures 2–3 de `7-ARC-NCA.pdf`.

## Slide 24 — Our Proposed NCA Solver

Tiempo: **1:30 minutos**.

Objetivo: responder directamente “How are you planning to solve this?”

> Based on this paper, our preliminary solver would follow five stages.

*Señala cada bloque conforme lo mencionas.*

> First, we take the demonstration pairs. Second, we encode each cell with visible color and hidden memory. Third, we train a small local update function for that task.
>
> Fourth, we run the automaton for different numbers of steps and random initializations, producing several candidates. Fifth, we verify every candidate against all demonstrations. A rule is useful only if it explains every example.

*Señala la ecuación, pero no leas cada símbolo.*

> The equation says: choose parameters that minimize the difference between the evolved grids and their targets. We then apply the same dynamics to the test input.
>
> We would also save intermediate states. The paper reports cases where the correct output appears during evolution, but the final state is wrong by one pixel. The best solution is not necessarily the last state.

Apoyo en el paper: Figure 4, la función `PixelWiseMSE` y la sección “Model Training” de `7-ARC-NCA.pdf`.

## Slide 25 — Where Can NCA Help—or Fail?

Tiempo: **1:15 minutos**.

Objetivo: mostrar pensamiento crítico y justificar una estrategia híbrida.

> NCA seems well suited to transformations that develop spatially: growing patterns, connecting points, propagating lines or filling bounded regions. Repeated local interactions can produce these operations.
>
> But global and symbolic problems may be difficult for a local model: counting disconnected objects, comparing distant regions or combining conditional rules. Resizing is also challenging because the automaton normally uses a fixed lattice. And ARC requires exact output, so one wrong pixel means failure.

*Señala la arquitectura híbrida inferior.*

> Therefore, we propose a hybrid: an object-centric stage identifies shapes and global relations, NCA generates spatial transformations, and a symbolic verifier rejects candidates that do not reproduce every demonstration.

> NCA is therefore our main experimental idea, but not a claim that local dynamics alone will solve the entire benchmark.

Apoyo en el paper: “Dealing with Changing Grid Size”, “Local versus Global Solutions”, “Almost Solved Problems” y “Reasoning Pitfalls” de `7-ARC-NCA.pdf`; representación object-centric de `Graphs, Constraints, and Search for the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus.pdf`.

## Slide 26 — How Will We Test the Idea?

Tiempo: **1:15 minutos**.

Objetivo: convertir el brainstorming en un experimento concreto y falsable.

> Our plan begins with a small experiment rather than a claim that we already have a complete solver.

> First, we classify tasks by transformation type, separating local growth and propagation from object-centric and compositional reasoning.
>
> Second, we test a simple NCA baseline on families where local dynamics make sense, such as filling, connection and pattern growth.
>
> Third, we measure exact task accuracy, stability across rollout lengths, computational cost and generalization—not only average pixel accuracy.
>
> Finally, we analyze failures. If NCA captures a spatial transformation but misses a global relation, we add object-level features or symbolic verification. The errors should determine which extra mechanism is necessary.

*Pausa brevemente antes de la pregunta final.*

> Our research question is: can abstract reasoning emerge from learned local dynamics, and can verification make those dynamics exact?

Apoyo en los papers: “Future Work” de `7-ARC-NCA.pdf` y el patrón de refinamiento observado en TRM y SOAR.
